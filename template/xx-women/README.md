# XX WOMEN Magazine Recipe

**XX WOMEN**  
*The Art of Being a Woman.*

A reusable premium mainstream women's lifestyle magazine recipe for MariCMS.

## Editorial identity

XX WOMEN is designed to look and feel like a contemporary national women's magazine. Its Christian worldview is an underlying editorial framework, not a visual genre.

The recipe supports fashion, beauty, home, food, wellness, relationships, money, work, travel, culture, faith, pro-life coverage, politics, voices and commerce.

The site should never be forced into a single subject identity. The lead story is selected by editorial importance and reader interest, regardless of department.

## Departments

Style · Beauty · Home · Food · Wellness · Love & Family · Money · Work · Travel · Culture · Faith · Pro-Life · Politics · Voices · Shop

## Editorial rules

- News and reporting are factual, sourced and clearly distinguished from commentary.
- Opinion is visibly labeled.
- Political coverage is descriptive and sourced, without campaign-style presentation.
- Health and financial content should identify sources and limitations where appropriate.
- Faith coverage can include Orthodox Christian material while remaining accessible to a mainstream audience.
- Product reviews and shopping content must distinguish editorial judgment from advertising or affiliate relationships.
- Sponsored content must be clearly labeled.

## Generational extensions

The recipe can later support:

- XX Tween — girlhood
- XX Teen — becoming
- XX Women — womanhood

These should share the XX editorial foundation but have distinct age-appropriate visual systems.

## Commercial model

Designed for fashion, beauty, jewelry, home, food, travel, wellness, finance, insurance, automotive, retail, hospitality, books and family advertisers.

Potential extensions:

website · social · newsletter · commerce · events · membership · premium print

## Design principle

Modern 2026 editorial design. Do not make the publication look retro, devotional, political or like a generic Christian blog.

The worldview lives in the editorial choices, not in crosses, church imagery, beige nostalgia or religious clichés.
